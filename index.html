How the naturalization test works

The test happens during your citizenship interview at a USCIS office. It has two parts, English and civics, and you can prepare for both in the app.

  1. Speaking

    The officer checks your spoken English while going over your N-400 application with you.

  2. Reading

    You read one of up to three sentences out loud. One correct sentence passes.

  3. Writing

    You write one of up to three sentences the officer reads to you.

  4. Civics

    The officer asks questions about U.S. history and government. You answer out loud.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers about the test and the app. For billing and account help, see the support page.

How many questions are on the 2025 civics test?

There are 128 questions to study. In the interview the officer asks you up to 20 of them, and you pass by answering 12 correctly. The officer stops as soon as you have 12 correct answers, or 9 wrong ones.

How does the 2008 civics test work?

You study 100 questions. The officer asks up to 10 of them, and you pass by answering 6 correctly.

Which version of the civics test will I take?

It depends on when you filed Form N-400. If you filed on or after October 20, 2025, you take the 2025 test. If you filed before that date, you take the 2008 test. You can switch versions any time in the app.

Is the app free?

The app is free to download and you can start studying right away. Some features are part of Premium, an optional subscription you manage and can cancel in your App Store or Google Play account.

Does the app work without internet?

Yes. The questions, answers and practice tests are on your phone, so you can study offline.

I am 65 or older. Do I need to study all the questions?

If you are 65 or older and have been a green card holder for at least 20 years, you only study a shorter list of 20 questions marked for 65/20 applicants, and you can take the civics test in the language of your choice. The app has this list.

Some answers depend on where I live. Does the app know?

Yes. Choose your state in the app's settings and the answers about your governor, senators and representative update.

Is Apponur part of USCIS?

No. Apponur is an independent developer and is not affiliated with or endorsed by USCIS or any government agency. The questions come from public USCIS study materials.
